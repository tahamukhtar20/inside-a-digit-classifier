    How it works

    The model

    Two 3 × 3 convolution layers (8 and 16 filters, each followed by max-pooling), a 64-unit hidden layer and 10 outputs: about 52,000 weights in all. It's trained on MNIST with random shifts, rotations and scaling, because hand-drawn digits are messier than the dataset.

    The explanations

    Saliency is the gradient of the chosen digit's score with respect to each pixel. SmoothGrad averages that gradient over 24 noisy copies of your drawing, which removes most of the speckle. Gradient × input keeps only the pixels you actually drew. Grad-CAM weights the second layer's maps by how much they matter and projects them back onto the image. With a comparison digit, everything explains the difference between the two scores.

    Your drawing

    Before it reaches the model, your digit is cropped, scaled to fit 20 × 20 pixels and centred by its centre of mass on a 28 × 28 grid, the same way MNIST was prepared. Nothing leaves your browser.
