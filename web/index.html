    What each layer knows

    Linear probes on frozen activations

    A linear probe is a simple model trained to read a property straight out of a layer's activations. The network was only ever trained to name digits. Whether its layers also encode a closed loop, the slant or the stroke thickness shows what it learned along the way.

    How it works

    The model

    Two 3 × 3 convolution layers (8 and 16 filters, each followed by max-pooling), a 64-unit hidden layer and 10 outputs: about 52,000 weights in all. It's trained on MNIST with random shifts, rotations and scaling, because hand-drawn digits are messier than the dataset.

    The explanations

    Saliency is the gradient of the chosen digit's score with respect to each pixel. SmoothGrad averages it over 24 noisy copies of your drawing. Gradient × input keeps only the pixels you drew. Grad-CAM weights the second layer's maps by how much they matter. With a comparison digit, everything explains the difference between the two scores.

    Units, ablation, probes

    Feature visualisations come from gradient ascent on a blank input. Ablation multiplies a unit's output by zero. Probes are logistic or linear models trained on 12,000 MNIST digits' activations and scored on 3,000 others. Your drawing is cropped, fitted to 20 × 20 and centred like MNIST.
